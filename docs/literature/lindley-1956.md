# Lindley, D. V. (1956)

- **Citation:** Lindley, D. V. (1956). On a measure of the information provided by an experiment. *The Annals of Mathematical Statistics*, 27(4), 986–1005.
- **Year:** 1956
- **Problem:** How to quantify the information an experiment provides for Bayesian inference.
- **Method:** Expected reduction in uncertainty (typically entropy) of a posterior, i.e. an information-theoretic expected utility.
- **Dataset/environment:** Theoretical; no scientific simulator.
- **Objective:** Information provided by an experiment.
- **Baseline:** Decision-theoretic alternatives (quadratic loss, etc.).
- **Limitations:** Assumes a specified parameter/model; does not define "scientific discovery" beyond posterior uncertainty.
- **Relevance to ECHO:** Formal ancestor of information-gain acquisition, including ECHO V0's expected knowledge change.
- **Potential research gap:** Whether expected posterior-entropy reduction is the right utility when the scientific target is a hidden mechanism rather than a pre-specified parameter.
