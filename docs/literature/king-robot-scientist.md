# King, R. D., et al. (2004, 2009)

- **Citation (2004):** King, R. D., Whelan, K. E., Jones, F. M., Reiser, P. G. K., Bryant, C. H., Muggleton, S. H., Kell, D. B., & Oliver, S. G. (2004). Functional genomic hypothesis generation and experimentation by a robot scientist. *Nature*, 427, 247–252.
- **Citation (2009):** King, R. D., Rowland, J., Oliver, S. G., Young, M., Aubrey, W., Byrne, E., Liakata, M., Markham, M., Pir, P., Soldatova, L. N., Sparkes, A., Whelan, K. E., & Clare, A. (2009). The Automation of Science. *Science*, 324(5923), 85–89.
- **Year:** 2004, 2009
- **Problem:** Closed-loop hypothesis generation and wet-lab testing for functional genomics (yeast).
- **Method:** Logical/abductive hypothesis generation plus laboratory automation ("robot scientist" Adam).
- **Dataset/environment:** Yeast functional genomics; physical experiments.
- **Objective:** Identify gene function / discriminate hypotheses with real assays.
- **Baseline:** Human experimenters / non-closed-loop designs in the original studies.
- **Limitations:** Domain-specific logical representations; not a general GP acquisition benchmark. Physical automation is not something ECHO should claim.
- **Relevance to ECHO:** Shows that sequential, hypothesis-driven experiment selection is an old scientific-automation idea. ECHO's discrete-hypothesis work (Experiment 2+) should cite this line rather than imply it is new.
- **Potential research gap:** Controlled, reproducible synthetic worlds where hypothesis-aware vs uncertainty-aware policies can be compared statistically, before any laboratory claim.
